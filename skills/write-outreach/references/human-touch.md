# Human-touch check: does this email read like a person wrote it?

Recipients don't separate "AI-written" from "lazy template". They react the same way to anything generic,
over-polished, or mass-produced. The real test: **could this have been sent to 500 people?** If yes, it fails.

## The 3 load-bearing gates (fail any → rewrite)

1. **Homework** — names ONE specific, verifiable thing about the recipient (a post, talk, repo, project, or JD line) AND what about it stood out. Not "loved your recent post": which post, what it said.
2. **Non-reusable opener** — the first sentence would break if pasted into an email to someone else.
3. **Clear, small ask** — one plain, low-friction ask ("worth 15 minutes next week?"). Not a templated CTA, not no ask.

Never fake gate 1. If there is no real hook, find one or don't send.

## Full checklist (pass ≥10/12, all 3 gates required)

1. Homework: specific verifiable reference to the recipient (gate)
2. Non-reusable opener (gate)
3. No banned openers ("I hope this finds you well", "I came across", "My name is…", "I noticed", "Dear Sir/Madam")
4. ≤2 corporate filler words (leverage, streamline, optimize, empower, unlock, elevate, robust, scalable, seamless, holistic, synergy, cutting-edge, end-to-end)
5. Contractions present (I'm, you're, I'd, don't)
6. Varied rhythm: at least one short sentence (<7 words) and one long one (>20 words)
7. ≤2 em-dashes total
8. No filler rule-of-three (three items where only one or two carry meaning)
9. A human edge: at least one of a specific opinion, a conversational aside, or a fragment
10. Concrete proof of fit: at least one specific outcome or metric tied to the recipient's need, not a resume dump
11. Clear, small ask (gate)
12. Brevity: under ~150 words for a first touch

## AI-tell phrases to avoid (a cluster = fail)

- **Openers:** "I hope this email finds you well", "My name is X and I'm", "I came across your profile/work", "I noticed you", "Dear Sir or Madam", "I've been following your company for a while"
- **Fake praise:** "I was impressed by your work", "I'm passionate about", "I'm so excited about", "your incredible / world-class / game-changing / innovative work"
- **Filler words:** leverage, streamline, maximize, optimize, empower, unlock, elevate, drive, enhance, robust, scalable, seamless, holistic, synergy, cutting-edge, end-to-end
- **Ask clichés:** "I'd love the opportunity to", "I'd love to connect / pick your brain", "touch base", "circle back", "just following up", "Are you the right person to speak with about this?", "align with your mission", "explore potential synergies", "I think there could be a great fit"
- **Over-formal:** "Thank you in advance for your time and consideration", "at your earliest convenience", "I would be delighted to", "Please don't hesitate to reach out"

The user's own banned phrases and hedges in `profile.md` (Writing preferences) apply on top of this list.

## What human looks like

- A real reason for emailing this person: a sentence that couldn't go to anyone else
- A genuine opinion or reaction; people have takes, templates stay neutral
- Contractions throughout; "you" and "I", not "users" or "the team"
- Varied rhythm: a 3-word sentence next to a 25-word one
- One fragment ("Two reasons.") or one aside ("honestly,"), used once, not five times
- One concrete metric tied to their need
- Plain text and a simple signature, like an ordinary one-to-one email
- **Read-aloud test:** if it sounds like a press release, it fails

## Before → after (fictional)

- Template: "I hope this email finds you well. My name is Sam, and I came across your profile and was really impressed by your work."
- Human: "Your write-up on flaky tests, especially tracking test stability as a team metric, is the clearest take I've read on it. That problem ate most of my last year."

- Template: "I'm passionate about leveraging cutting-edge AI to streamline workflows and unlock value, and I'd love to connect and explore potential synergies."
- Human: "I build document pipelines that hold up under load; the last one cut processing time by about 40%. Your JD's focus on scaling evaluation is exactly the problem I'd want to work on."

- Template: "Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call to explore this further? Thank you in advance for your time and consideration."
- Human: "Worth 15 minutes next week to see if I'd be useful to the team?"

Any metric in a real draft must come from `profile.md`; the numbers above are illustrations only.

## Caveat

These are perception heuristics, not proof of authorship. A skilled human trips some of them too.
Weigh clusters, not single hits. Gates 1, 2, and 11 carry the weight; the rest is texture.
